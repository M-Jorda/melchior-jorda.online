Epic is moving on from Blueprints, Cisco and OpenAI are betting on eBPF to contain vulnerabilities, and a 27-billion-parameter model now fits in 5.9 GB. On the security side, trailer brakes can be triggered over Bluetooth without authentication.

## Key points

::: details **Unreal Engine 6** : Epic announced on November 23 that UE6 will ship without Blueprints or the Actor framework.
Epic published the post [“An update on Blueprints and the path to UE6”](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/news/an-update-on-blueprints-and-the-path-to-ue6). Two long-standing building blocks of the engine are going away:

- **Blueprints**, the node-based visual scripting system widely used by game designers and artists;
- the **Actor** framework, game objects written in C++ and extended with components.

The replacement is Verse, Epic's in-house language already used in the Fortnite editor. It also relies on Scene Graph, which organizes the world as a tree of entities built from components. In June, Epic was still promising that both would coexist in early UE6 releases.

Epic attributes the shift to AI, which should help convert UE5 projects, “or even make the process fully automatic”. According to [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/unreal-engine-6-to-remove-blueprints-and-actor-gameplay-framework-from-the-beginning), projects already in production can stay on UE5.

Reactions reported by the press ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28098/epic-games-will-remove-blueprints-and-actors-from-unreal-engine-6)) show what is at stake:

- Sandfall says Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 is built “95%” in Blueprints;
- former Valve writer Chet Faliszek says his team will stay on UE5.

Several points remain uncertain: the timeline, the quality of migration tools, how long UE5 will be maintained, and whether a new visual tool will appear.

> **For you:** if you are starting an Unreal project, watch how long UE5 will be maintained and what migration tools Epic releases before choosing between Blueprints and Verse.
:::

::: details **Shirudo** : Cisco and OpenAI release an open source tool that blocks Linux exploits with eBPF programs until a patch lands.
Cisco and OpenAI release [Shirudo](https://github.com/shirudo-dev/shirudo), an open source tool under the BSD license, covered by [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/24/cisco-and-openai-pitch-ebpf-kernel-plugins-as-the-way-to-tame-the-patchpocalypse/5311795). Cisco owns Isovalent, the company behind the eBPF tools Cilium and Tetragon.

The idea is to put a compensating control in the kernel before the real fix:

- AI agents find the vulnerabilities;
- Codex writes a small eBPF program that blocks the attack;
- a human approves it before production.

The repository describes signed micro-patches that only authorized loaders can add. A TPM 2.0 chip attests which patches are active. A modified kernel is required.

The context: more than 100,000 vulnerabilities reported this year, a record driven by AI-assisted bug hunting. Both companies say they already neutralized recent Java flaws this way, including on Elasticsearch, with no downtime.

“Virtual patching” already exists at VMware, Palo Alto Networks and Rapid7. Two questions remain open: can a human really review shields produced at machine speed, and will the modified kernel slow adoption?

> **For you:** if you run Linux servers, read the repository's prerequisites (modified kernel, TPM 2.0, signed loaders) before planning a trial.
:::

::: details **Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B** : PrismML releases a ternary 27-billion-parameter model that fits in 5.9 GB.
PrismML releases [Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B](https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-5-27b) under the Apache 2.0 license. It is derived from Qwen3.8 27B, an open model from Alibaba.

Each weight is -1, 0 or +1, with a shared scale factor per group of 128 weights ([family overview](https://prismml.com/news/ternary-bonsai)). That works out to about 1.58 bits per weight, and multiplications become additions.

Figures announced by PrismML:

- size: 5.9 GB, about 9 times smaller than the original;
- SWE-bench Verified: 75.6% of bugs resolved;
- original model: 80.6%;
- previous Bonsai version: 60.8%;
- speed: about 204 tokens per second on an RTX 4090.

The model targets agents, meaning multi-step tasks that use tools. It runs on llama.cpp (NVIDIA, Intel) and MLX (Mac, iPhone, iPad).

Caution: every figure comes from PrismML, and the method is deferred to a white paper. The model remains slightly weaker than the original on most benchmarks, and slightly behind Bonsai 2 on LiveCodeBench.

> **For you:** to evaluate the ternary approach on an ordinary machine, you can try the 1.7-billion-parameter version published in GGUF on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-1.7B-gguf).
:::

::: details **Elecbrakes brakes** : four bytes sent over Bluetooth are enough to trigger trailer brakes, with no authentication at all.
Patrick Forck and Jeremy McCarthy of Element Digital [examined](https://elementdigital.com.au/blog/brake-check-trailer-brakes-over-bluetooth/) Elecbrakes trailer brake controllers. It started when a remote connected on its own to a stranger's trailer.

Their findings:

- 47 BLE characteristics exposed, 5 of them hidden from the official app;
- a braking command with no authentication, triggered with 4 bytes;
- no encryption and no replay protection;
- firmware checked only by a CRC32, with the decryption key inside the app.

Their hardware: a laptop running Chrome. They used Claude to analyze the Android app.

CISA published [advisory ICSA-26-323-02](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-323-02), listing three vulnerabilities. The two most severe score 9.6 out of 10. Timeline:

- report: May 18;
- fix for EB2 and VM1: July 6;
- disclosure: November 19.

The older ELBC2000 model has no fix, and updates are manual. Elecbrakes says it reproduced the flaw, but claims it does not exist when the owner is connected as the manual instructs ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/23/bluetooth-brakes-are-broken-as-researchers-find-anyone-can-control-them/5311748)).

> **For you:** if you build BLE devices, require encrypted pairing for any command that acts on the physical world, and sign your firmware instead of relying on a CRC32.
:::

### AI and research

::: details **Matrix multiplication** : OpenAI claims the exponent ω is exactly 9/4, or 2.25.
On November 24, OpenAI published in its [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) repository a [paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf) adding a lower bound: nothing can beat 2.25. The upper bound, published in early October, was checked by outside researchers ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28627)). The Lean verification covers a narrower setting than the claim, and the paper is not peer reviewed. According to the repository, most of these texts come from an unreleased internal model ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/openai-says-it-has-pinned-down-the-exact-speed-limit-for-matrix-multiplication-9-4/)). GPUs still use the schoolbook method or Strassen's.
:::

::: details **GLM-5.4** : Z.ai runs part of GLM-5's layers twice to build a coding-oriented model.
In a “Loop Transformer”, a group of layers is traversed several times with the same weights: the model computes more deeply without growing. Z.ai applies this to 28 layers of GLM-5. The idea dates back to 2018. Z.ai admits GLM-5.4 sometimes gets stuck in overly long reasoning. The weights are not published yet ([Next](https://next.ink/269432/glm-5-4-avec-loop-transformer-z-ai-propose-un-modele-pour-le-code-plus-generaliste/), [Z.ai blog](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4)).
:::

::: details **Riemann hypothesis** : the Clay Institute marks the problem “under review” after the proof obtained with Claude.
Levent Alpöge, a researcher at Anthropic, claims to have proved this 1859 conjecture with Claude.

- Clay requires publication, two years of review and agreement among mathematicians.
- The proof spans about 17 million lines of Lean, judged correct by the Mathlib team, but nobody yet understands why it works.
- Alpöge calls his figure of 250,000 pages of explanations a “joke”.
- A Stanford researcher circulated a human proof of about 1,000 pages, not yet verified.

Sources: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/24/anthropic-says-claude-solved-an-age-old-math-problem-but-the-written-proof-is-250000-pages-of-slop/5311775), [Next](https://next.ink/269118/hypothese-de-riemann-une-preuve-generee-par-ia-est-elle-utile-aux-mathematiciens/).
:::

::: details **GPT-6 and proteins** : OpenAI shows proteins designed through reasoning, 11% of which bind their target.
GPT-6 models design proteins by reasoning and writing code, without specialized biology tools for the design step. In the lab, 35 of 322 proteins bound their target ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/)). Limits: specialized tools were used to filter candidates. The model remains very poor at predicting a protein's full structure. OpenAI also acknowledges misuse risks.
:::

::: details **Quantum circuits in an LLM** : quantum circuits replace eight layers of Qwen3, with no demonstrated benefit.
Multiverse Computing and the University of Toronto replaced those layers (about 5 billion parameters) with 32 circuits of 8 qubits ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/24/researchers-fit-quantum-circuits-into-a-30-billion-parameter-ai-model/)). Results are nearly as good, but mostly in classical simulation. On an IBM chip, one token takes about 90 seconds. A small classical network of similar size does better, and removing entanglement changes nothing. Preprint, not peer reviewed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15250)).
:::

### Security and reliability

::: details **Check Point** : the “GT13” campaign hit at least 22 organizations through a flaw patched in July.
According to [GreyNoise](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/check-point-security-management-servers-intrusion-campaign), confirmed attacks ran from October 17 to November 2. They hit 17 countries, including France.

- Entry through a forgotten HFS server, then a forged admin session derived from the random generator's state.
- Exploitation of CVE-2026-16232, a console authentication bypass ([Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/etr-cve-2026-16232-critical-check-point-smartconsole-authentication-bypass-exploited-in-the-wild/)).
- Theft of firewall databases and internal certificate authority files.

GreyNoise suspects AI agents based on the pace of actions, without proof, and believes the campaign is ongoing.
:::

::: details **Microsoft 365 outage** : the final report traces the global outage to a defect installed three months earlier.
The [report](https://next.ink/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/pir_microsoft_365_11-12-2026.pdf) describes an outage of about 24 hours and its chain of causes:

- a Windows defect load-tested on older hardware, worse on newer machines;
- Teams maintenance, capacity reduced by 14% and the North American morning peak;
- load balancing that spread the failure to other regions.

Microsoft rules out a cyberattack and any change written by AI. The report lists 13 measures, most planned by early 2027 ([Next](https://next.ink/269535/panne-microsoft-plus-precis-le-rapport-final-nie-toute-cyberattaque-ou-usage-de-lia/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/23/microsoft-mega-outage-report-reveals-testing-gaps-and-emails-that-never-arrived/5311656)).
:::

::: details **Trapped coding agent** : according to Jesta, an OpenCode agent targeting a financial firm was steered into decoys.
On November 16, the attacker used a Mac, Chrome and curl. According to [Jesta's account](https://jesta.ai/blog/vibeoffense), the agent followed a link that only existed in a decoy document. It was spotted within 64 seconds. It gave up after 5 minutes 44, with nothing stolen. The key it found was fake, despite what [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/24/an-ai-coding-agent-a-browser-and-a-mac-tool-found-a-financial-firms-access-key-in-under-6-minutes/5311922) headline suggests. Caution: the account comes from a vendor and cannot be verified by third parties.
:::

### Web and browsers

::: details **Firefox 161** : the built-in VPN can now be toggled per site.
Released on November 24, Firefox 161 adds a configurable keyboard shortcut to copy the tab's address. A menu also appears for tab groups. The release fixes several security vulnerabilities ([release notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/161.0/releasenotes/), [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1109478/)).
:::

::: details **WebKit and the DMA** : no European iOS browser uses an engine other than WebKit, an open letter complains.
Mozilla, Brave, Vivaldi, Kagi and business associations ask the European Commission to open proceedings ([Next](https://next.ink/269516/malgre-le-dma-webkit-reste-le-seul-moteur-des-rivaux-de-safari-en-europe/)). They target Apple's conditions: special authorization, an EU-based publisher, and a separate EU-only app. According to the signatories, a more open web would save European businesses 11 billion euros a year.
:::

::: details **Safari beyond 60 frames per second** : a developer explains how to get around this limit on iPhone.
Safari caps requestAnimationFrame and touch events at 60 per second, despite 120 Hz screens. The [author](https://rida.dev/blog/bypassing-ios-safari-60-fps-limit) places an invisible scrolling area under the finger, handled by a separate thread. He then draws with OffscreenCanvas and reaches 118 frames per second on average. He advises against building an app on it, since Apple can break the trick at any time.
:::

### Low level, embedded and hardware

::: details **ST VL53LB** : STMicroelectronics unveils its smallest time-of-flight distance sensor.
Specs ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/24/st-vl53lb-is-the-smallest-flightsense-tof-ranging-sensor-at-just-4-0-x-1-8-x-1-0-mm/)):

- size: 4 × 1.8 × 1 mm, about 30% smaller than the previous generation;
- range: from 5 mm to over 8 m;
- in full sunlight: 1.1 m;
- I²C interface.

Target uses: camera autofocus, robot vacuums, coffee machines. General availability is planned for Q2 2027.
:::

::: details **TinyWifiDongle** : an Arduino library turns an ESP32-S3 or a Pico 2 W into a USB Wi-Fi adapter.
Written by Phil Schatzmann under the MIT license ([source code](https://github.com/pschatzmann/TinyWifiDongle)), it exposes the board as a USB CDC-NCM network card, recognized without drivers by Linux, macOS and Windows 11 ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/24/tinywifidongle-arduino-library-turns-esp32-s3-and-raspberry-pi-pico-2-w-boards-into-usb-wifi-adapters/)). Throughput:

- about 5 Mbit/s with an ESP32-S3;
- about 2 Mbit/s with a Pico 2 W.

USB full speed caps out at 12 Mbit/s. The author reports stalls after 100 to 200 MB of transfer.
:::

::: details **Tendryl** : Hellbender sells a prototyping AI camera built around a Raspberry Pi CM5.
Priced at 599 dollars, it combines ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/25/compact-tendryl-edge-ai-camera-packs-raspberry-pi-cm5-hailo-8-npu-12mp-sensor-speaker-and-microphone/)):

- a Hailo-8 NPU rated at 26 TOPS;
- a 12-megapixel sensor;
- a microphone, a speaker and Ethernet with power over the cable.

The software is based on Raspberry Pi OS. The promised GitHub repository only holds the system image for now.
:::

::: details **Arm documentation** : Arm demands the shutdown of a site that republishes its instruction set in readable form.
Since 2022, Jon Palmisciano has published a clean version of the documentation with fast search, built from Arm's official files ([the site](https://arm.jonpalmisc.com/)). Arm's lawyers cite copyright and trademark, with a December 4 deadline ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/24/arm-strongarms-site-that-hosts-ad-free-documentation-and-it-may-disappear/5311925)). Arm does not dispute the site's quality and says it wants to “engage”. The author is considering releasing his tools so anyone can generate a local copy.
:::

::: details **Linux 7.4-rc4** : Linus Torvalds calls this test release “huge”, but made of small fixes.
Released on November 23, rc4 puts half of its changes in drivers ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1108900/)). The same day, Greg Kroah-Hartman released seven stable kernels. They range from 7.3.6 to 5.10.275: older branches stay maintained for years ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1108909/)).
:::

::: details **SoftBank and the RAI Institute** : SoftBank buys the robotics lab founded by Marc Raibert from Hyundai.
Founded in 2022 by the creator of Boston Dynamics, the institute had received over 400 million dollars from Hyundai. The price was not disclosed. Based near Boston and in Zurich, it works on walking, grasping and perception. Separately, China blocked part of SoftBank's acquisition of ABB Robotics, planned at 5.4 billion dollars ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/softbank-acquires-marc-raiberts-robotics-and-ai-institute/)).
:::

### Gaming, careers and industry

::: details **GTA VI on PC** : the Leonida project boots a leaked Xbox build through static recompilation.
Static recompilation translates the whole console program ahead of time, whereas an emulator imitates the console in real time. It works partly because Xbox and Windows share a lot, including DirectX. The result is slow and crashes often. According to the author, Rockstar had his GitHub repository and Discord taken down, which is unconfirmed. Fake “PC ports” bundled with spyware are circulating ([Next](https://next.ink/269712/gta-vi-demarre-sur-pc-avant-meme-lannonce-dun-portage-officiel/)).
:::

::: details **Quest Virtual Display** : Meta's app passes the headset's camera and microphone to the PC and supports USB.
The Quest's cameras can act as a webcam, for example in Zoom, and its microphone as the PC's microphone. A USB cable can replace poor Wi-Fi. The PC's mouse and keyboard can also drive the headset interface. Some features require the Windows 11 Mixed Reality Link app ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/quests-virtual-display-gets-camera-microphone-usb-input-upgrades/)).
:::

::: details **Junior hiring** : US data shows fewer young developers, without blaming AI alone.
Rufus Pollock ([DataHub](https://datahub.io/blog/junior-dev-hiring)) finds:

- nearly a fifth fewer developers aged 22 to 25 since November 2022, in the companies tracked;
- growth in every group over 30;
- Indeed developer postings 18% below their February 2020 level;
- about 60% of the decline happening before ChatGPT.

Interest rates, 2021 over-hiring and a US tax rule also weigh in. The data shows “when and who”, not “why”.
:::

::: details **BepiColombo** : the probe is orbiting Mercury, but on an orbit that is too wide after a software bug.
Arriving on November 21, the probe hit a software issue in attitude and thruster control that cut thrust too early:

- about 77% of the planned braking;
- a 75-hour orbit instead of 58.

The probe is stable. The bug must be fixed remotely before its two orbiters separate, planned for December ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/bepicolombo/latest-updates-bepicolombo-s-arrival-at-mercury), [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/missions/esa-jaxa-bepicolomobo-mission-mercury-orbit-arrival)).
:::

## Analysis

Two announcements put AI at the heart of existing pipelines: Epic counts on it to convert UE5 projects, and Shirudo has Codex write eBPF shields. In both cases, the quality of validation becomes the critical point. It remains to be seen whether human review can keep up with machine speed, a question raised explicitly for Shirudo.

The Elecbrakes, Check Point and Microsoft 365 cases point to general practices rather than a specific technology. They come down to authenticating sensitive commands, applying available patches and testing on hardware close to production.

Finally, several results rest on their authors' own figures: Ternary Bonsai's scores, Jesta's account and OpenAI's 9/4 bound. Independent verification is the next thing to watch.
