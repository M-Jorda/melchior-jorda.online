The final draft of C++26 has been rejected, PostgreSQL ships an unusually large security update and llama.cpp reaches version 1.0. Meanwhile, the number of vulnerabilities published in 2026 has passed 100,000.

## Key points

::: details **C++26** : national bodies rejected the final draft of the standard, mainly because of "contracts".
Contracts let code state what must be true: preconditions, postconditions and `contract_assert(...)`. The adopted proposal, P2900, aims to catch errors before they turn into undefined behavior.

On October 23, the final draft (DIS) failed, apparently a first for a C++ version. According to [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/devops/2026/11/13/c26-stumbles-at-finish-line-over-contracts-dispute/5309526):

- 13 votes in favor, 9 against, 12 abstentions;
- ISO allows at most 25% of votes against;
- here, 9 out of 22 voters, or 41%.

[Andreas Fertig](https://andreasfertig.com/blog/2026/11/cpp26-dis-failed-how-does-cpp-survive), a committee member, notes that the vote breakdown is not necessarily published. These figures should therefore be treated with caution.

Bjarne Stroustrup and co-authors call this version "an existential threat": too complex, poorly tested, better suited to a library. Andreas Fertig points out that it won several majority votes and that rejecting it this late damages trust in the process. Contracts had already been pulled from C++20 in 2019.

The committee meets in Brazil from November 16. The options on the table: drop contracts, move them to a technical specification, or fix them and vote again. The standard may slip to 2027, while part of the standard library hardening already relies on contracts.

> **For you:** wait for the outcome of the committee meeting in Brazil before planning to use contracts in a C++ project.
:::

::: details **PostgreSQL** : 52 vulnerabilities fixed on November 12, including a type confusion dating back to 2004 that allows code execution on the server.
Versions 18.7, 17.12, 16.16, 15.20 and 14.25 fix 52 vulnerabilities and more than 140 bugs, according to the [official announcement](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-187-1712-1616-1520-1425-and-19-rc-2-released-3442/). It is an unusually heavy update.

The most severe, CVE-2026-6460, scores 8.8 out of 10. A user who can create objects can run code with the server's privileges. The idea: prepare a query that holds a value, change the type of one field, then run the query, which reads the old value with the new type.

According to researcher Mehmet Ince, quoted by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/postgresql-patches-52-flaws-including.html), the root cause dates back to May 5, 2004. In August he alerted AWS, Microsoft, Google, Supabase, Databricks and Ubicloud. No real-world attack has been reported.

Other points:

- 32 of the 52 flaws score 8.8;
- only one can be triggered without an account, and it restarts version 18 servers;
- PostgreSQL 14 receives its final update;
- PostgreSQL 19 is expected on November 19.

Shared hosting services, which grant object-creation rights, are the most exposed. There is no workaround. According to package maintainers, C extensions must be rebuilt along with the server.

> **For you:** update your instances, rebuild C extensions and follow the announcement's post-update steps, including rebuilding some indexes.
:::

::: details **llama.cpp 1.0** : the open-source local inference engine and its ggml library reach version 1.0.0, with a stable C API.
llama.cpp, written in C and C++, runs large language models on a computer or even a phone. It relies on quantization (weights stored on 4 or 8 bits instead of 16) and on its own GGUF file format.

Version 1.0.0 shipped on November 13, after roughly three and a half years of development. It brings:

- a C interface now considered stable: any breaking change will require a new major version;
- removal of 35 deprecated functions;
- support for more than 150 model architecture variants, on NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm, Arm, IBM and Intel hardware;
- security protections and text-to-speech in the built-in server;
- packages for Linux, macOS, Windows, Android and iOS.

Many consumer tools for local AI are built on llama.cpp. A stable API keeps them from breaking with every update. The team says it uses LLMs for development while keeping control of the architecture and understanding of the code as priorities. Details in the [ggml team's post](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llama-cpp-v1) and the [release notes](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/v1.0.0).

> **For you:** before upgrading, check that your code calls none of the 35 removed deprecated functions.
:::

::: details **100,000 CVEs in 2026** : the milestone was crossed on November 13, a rise attributed to AI-assisted vulnerability research.
According to VulnCheck, quoted by [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/13/this-years-bug-count-just-passed-100k/5309882), 100,222 CVEs have been published in 2026. That is more than double the whole of 2025. Key figures:

- about 1.4% (1,363) have been exploited;
- the median time to exploitation fell from 120 days in 2025 to 80 days in early 2026;
- according to CrowdStrike, 88% of exploitations using public code happen within 48 hours.

Josh Bressers of VulnCheck sums it up: "It's AI." CISA now asks organizations to prioritize by internet exposure and real-world exploitation, not just by severity score.

On the Linux kernel side, [Lorenzo Stoakes](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/13/another-linux-kernel-developer-steps-back-from-some-duties-due-to-overload/5309628), an Arm engineer who maintains four parts of memory management, is stopping reviews outside his areas. He says AI-generated patch series now make up "half of what I see". Greg Kroah-Hartman observes: "We don't have enough people to review everything."

Linus Torvalds said on November 9 that he saw signs of a peak, which remains uncertain. Both articles come from the same outlet, so caution applies.

> **For you:** triage alerts by KEV listing and service exposure rather than by the score out of 10 alone.
:::

### Security

::: details **Mior and Skyfall** : two attacks let a malicious host bypass SEV-SNP, AMD's confidential computing technology.
- Mior (ETH Zurich): at boot, the security processor's writes are redirected to a graphics card, leaving part of the RMP table empty. The hypervisor can then read a VM's memory or get a fake attestation signed. It succeeded 1,000 times out of 1,000 on an EPYC 9135, requires BIOS control, and scores 5.9.
- Skyfall (ETH Zurich and Google): recovery of a disk encryption key on Zen 3 and Zen 4, score 4.9.

AMD has released microcode and firmware fixes. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-mior-and-skyfall-attacks-let-rogue.html) · [Mior page](https://xca-attacks.github.io/mior/)
:::

::: details **Operation Endgame** : police dismantle the infrastructure of Gootloader, CastleRAT and StealC.
Europol and European and US police targeted a ransomware loader, a remote access tool and a password stealer. Results:

- 235 servers and 257 domains seized;
- 6.4 million credentials recovered, belonging to more than 40,000 victims.

Gootloader hid in fake legal documents on hacked WordPress sites. No arrests were announced. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/operation-endgame-takes-down-gootloader.html)
:::

::: details **Tampered Trezor wallets** : three Safe 5 devices contained a hidden spy circuit board.
The devices came from CryptoBilis, a former official reseller in Southeast Asia. The board, placed between the main board and the screen, copied the displayed recovery phrase. It then sent it over the mobile network. This is a supply chain attack: the product is compromised before reaching the buyer. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/trezor-confirms-hidden-hardware.html)

At least 7,870 people are affected in three US states, and the total is expected to grow. After Notion, it is another leak through a supplier's supplier. ## Analysis

For C and C++ developers, the C++26 setback extends the wait for a standard contracts mechanism, at a time when the language's safety is under fire and some projects are moving to Rust. llama.cpp, for its part, shows that a widely used C/C++ project can freeze a public API and commit to semantic versioning.

On the back end, the PostgreSQL update affects any application built on this database, shared hosting in particular. NEXUS, Melchior JORDA's self-hosted personal assistant, uses PostgreSQL and is among the projects concerned.

The 100,000-CVE milestone and overloaded Linux maintainers point to the same bottleneck: finding flaws is speeding up, while reviewing them remains human work. It remains to be seen whether the peak Linus Torvalds mentioned holds. It also remains to be seen whether C++26 can ship without contracts, which part of the standard library hardening already relies on.
