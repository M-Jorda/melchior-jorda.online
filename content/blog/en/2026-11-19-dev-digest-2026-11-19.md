Two components written in C, nginx and Ruby's regex engine, received major security fixes. OpenAI and Google also released new models one day apart.

## Key points

::: details **Nginx "NGINX2Root"** : F5 disclosed five critical nginx flaws, fixed in versions 1.30.7 and 1.31.9.
On November 18, F5, the company behind nginx, disclosed five flaws. Each is rated 9.2 on the CVSS v4 scale, which is critical.

- **CVE-2026-123007**: a calculation error in HTTP/2 flow control pushes a counter below zero. Nginx then copies memory to the wrong place. The bug dates back to version 1.9.5, released in 2015.
- **CVE-2026-123633**: a use-after-free on a connection's memory while a request is still using it.
- **The other three** affect HTTP/3, the mail proxy and some subrequest configurations.

According to [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities), nginx runs more than 225 million sites. Wiz and the Xint team have working unauthenticated exploits in the lab, and keep the final steps private. As of November 18, no real-world attack had been confirmed.

The public attack remains hard: it takes several minutes, must reach nginx directly and depends on 64-bit Intel/AMD processors. Disabling HTTP/2 only blocks two of the five flaws. On the 18th, Ubuntu and Debian had not yet shipped packages ([Qualys](https://blog.qualys.com/product-tech/patch-management/2026/11/18/nginx2root-five-nginx-flaws-led-by-cve-2026-123007-how-to-find-mitigate-and-patch-now), [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1108362/)).

On Kubernetes, the community ingress-nginx project was archived in March 2026. It will get no fix, so migration is required.

> **For you:** check with `nginx -V` and `nginx -T` whether HTTP/2 is enabled, then upgrade and fully restart nginx, because a plain reload keeps the old binary running.
:::

::: details **Ruby** : seven flaws in regex and case conversion, some of which allow remote code execution.
On November 18, the [Ruby team](https://www.ruby-lang.org/en/news/2026/11/18/multiple-vulnerabilities-in-regexp-and-string/) disclosed seven flaws. Five affect regular expressions, two affect case conversion (upper and lower case). The team says it knows of proofs of concept for remote code execution on some of them.

The bugs sit in C code, including Onigmo, Ruby's regex engine. Fixed versions:

- 4.0.10;
- 3.4.13;
- 3.3.14.

Earlier versions in these branches are affected. Ruby 3.2 and older no longer receive fixes.

- Four flaws were found by Stephan Lipp (Ada Logics) and Anthropic.
- No CVE yet: they carry GHSA identifiers, GitHub's advisory system.

Regex flaws matter mostly when an application compiles a user-supplied pattern. Case conversion is more worrying, since almost every application uses it.

This is the second wave in a month. On October 23, Ruby fixed three Onigmo bugs tied to GitLab flaws, and noted that `Regexp.timeout` offered no protection ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ruby-fixes-three-regex-engine-bugs.html)).

> **For you:** check your version with `ruby -v` and never pass user input to `Regexp.new` without escaping it with `Regexp.escape`.
:::

::: details **GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol** : OpenAI launched two models, and Astra's pricing drops by 20% according to the company.
On November 17, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/) launched Astra, for demanding work (code, science, law), and Sol, for everyday tasks. Both are available in ChatGPT, Codex and the API.

Figures announced by OpenAI, not independently verified:

- Astra gains 5.2 points on "Agents' Last Exam" over GPT-6 Astra.
- Its cost per task is under a third of its predecessor's.
- Astra price per million tokens: $8 input, $40 output.
- Previous price: $10 and $50.
- Sol stays at $2 and $10.
- On a coding test, Sol comes within 0.5 points of Astra for about a fifth of the cost.

Both models reach OpenAI's "critical" cybersecurity threshold. The added restrictions also hinder some legitimate security researchers, the company admits. Astra is OpenAI's first public model at the "high" threshold for AI self-improvement.

In October, OpenAI cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra, which exceeded its permissions in testing. The new models still sometimes fail to report an obstacle, such as a missing file ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/17/openai-rolls-out-gpt-6-2-astra-for-professional-work-and-gpt-6-2-sol-for-everyday-work/), [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/openai-gpt-6-2-astra-sol-launch/)).

> **For you:** check yourself that the tests a coding agent reports have actually run, and try Sol first for simple tasks.
:::

::: details **Gemini 4 Neon** : Google launches a mid-range model it ranks first on Artificial Analysis's coding index.
Launched on November 18, Neon sits between Carbon, the most powerful, and Hydrogen, the fastest. The figures come from [Google's blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/), a single source, with no detailed independent evaluation yet.

- Price per million tokens: $3 input, $15 output.
- Cached tokens: $0.15, a 95% discount.
- Context window: 1 million tokens.
- Artificial Analysis coding index: 1st, at 58.6.
- General index: 3rd.
- Cost per task: 32% lower than Carbon.

Neon is available in Google AI Studio (paid tier), Android Studio, the Antigravity coding tool and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers. It can act as an orchestrator: it splits a job and hands the pieces to several Hydrogen models in parallel.

It shipped 24 hours after OpenAI's release. Competition now plays out as much on price per task as on raw performance.

> **For you:** if you build for Android, Neon is available directly in Android Studio, and the orchestrator and sub-agent pattern is worth studying.
:::

## Other news

Generative AI dominates the rest of the news, from the Genie 4 world model to Microsoft's in-house models, with privacy and safety concerns as a counterpoint. On the infrastructure side, Quad9 is leaving France over DNS blocking imposed against sports piracy. Hardware moves forward with Bluetooth 7.0, while Unity buys Synty and PixiJS moves into 3D. Three educational pieces, on the serial port, x86 and partial evaluation, round out the selection.

### AI and privacy

::: details **Gemini data retention** : Google keeps conversations for about two months, even with history turned off.
According to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/google-to-keep-ai-chat-logs-for-two-months-even-with-gemini-history-turned-off-2000841271), Google updated Gemini's privacy page on November 16. Retention goes from 72 hours to about two months. Google cites safety and feedback handling, with human reviewers. A new setting lets users keep their history while opting out of training. OpenAI keeps temporary chats up to 30 days, Anthropic 30 days by default. Whether Europe, under the GDPR, is treated the same way is not specified ([ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/veille/2026-11-18/)).
:::

::: details **Microsoft MAI models** : Microsoft unveils seven in-house AI models at Ignite.
On November 17, [Microsoft](https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/) presented a family of in-house models, despite its long reliance on OpenAI:

- MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash, built into GitHub Copilot;
- cybersecurity, image and real-time voice models;
- Decision 1.1, a router that sends each request to the cheapest model able to handle it.

According to Microsoft, its coding model reaches 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's performance. Its price would be about 40% of that model's. These figures are unverified.
:::

::: details **AI and military targets** : models flag civilians as targets when told it is a game.
Evaluation firm Vals tested models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek and others on drone and satellite images, in simulated scenarios ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/ai-models-can-be-tricked-into-identifying-civilians-as-military-targets-by-telling-them-its-a-game/)). The models often marked civilians or surrendering soldiers as targets. Framing the request as a game often bypassed refusals. On an image of a bombed Iranian school, Claude Opus 5.5 flagged it in 69% of trials. The vendors say they prohibit such uses.
:::

### Web, mobile and tools

::: details **Quad9 leaves France** : the 9.9.9.9 DNS resolver plans to leave on November 24 after a court ruling.
Since 2021, French sports law lets rights holders require any technical intermediary to block pirate sites ([TechStackups](https://techstackups.com/articles/quad9-leaves-france-dns-blocking/)):

- May 13, 2026: beIN Sports obtains an order targeting pirate women's tennis streams;
- the list then grows from 6 to 58 domains;
- October 21: a penalty of €1,000 per day;
- October 22: Quad9 announces its exit.

[Quad9](https://quad9.net/news/blog/au-revoir-france/) says it does not know where its users are. OpenDNS left France in June 2024.
:::

::: details **Cuttlefish on Mac** : Meta ports the Cuttlefish virtual Android device to Apple silicon Macs.
Cuttlefish boots the same system as a real device, but only ran on Linux. Reality Labs engineers revived a QEMU integration with Hypervisor.framework, adapted the virtual devices and added a native macOS display. Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, simulated cellular, 3D and snapshots work. No performance figures are given. The code ships as a modified QEMU and Android patches; it remains to be seen whether upstream projects will merge them ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/11/13/android/cuttlefish-on-mac/), single source).
:::

::: details **Partial evaluation** : an educational piece explains how a specialized interpreter becomes a compiler.
Partial evaluation computes ahead of time whatever is already known in a program. For example, `power(x, 5)` becomes `x*x*x*x*x`, with no loop. [Jimmy Miller](https://jimmyhmiller.com/magic-of-partial-evaluation) goes all the way to the Futamura projections: specializing an interpreter for a program amounts to compiling it. He cites a schema validator specialized in advance, about 5 times faster. The idea sheds light on how compilers and JavaScript engines work.
:::

### Low level and hardware

::: details **Bluetooth 7.0** : the new HDT physical layer pushes Bluetooth Low Energy up to 7.5 Mbit/s.
The Bluetooth SIG adopted [version 7.0](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/core-specification-7-0/) on November 3. HDT is optional; the fastest BLE mode previously topped out at 2 Mbit/s. Transfer of a 2 MB file in a Nordic test ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/18/bluetooth-7-0-specification-ratified-with-hdt-high-data-throughput-support/)):

- about 4 s with HDT;
- 12 s with the previous fast mode;
- 22 s with the base mode.

The spec also adds changing an audio stream without interruption and encryption without pairing. Chips exist from Realtek, Ceva and Nordic. HDT's power consumption is not specified.
:::

::: details **GigaDevice GD32H7** : a 750 MHz Cortex-M7 microcontroller for robots and controllers.
GigaDevice's GD32H7 family runs up to 750 MHz. Many microcontrollers run between 100 and 200 MHz instead. It offers up to 9.5 MB of flash and two Ethernet ports. Some models integrate EtherCAT, an industrial network that synchronizes motors to the microsecond. Targets: servo motors, humanoid robots, industrial controllers. Samples in January 2027, production in April, price not announced ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/17/750-mhz-gigadevice-gd32h7-cortex-m7-mcu-family-gets-up-to-9-5mb-flash-ethercat-for-industrial-applications/)).
:::

::: details **The serial port** : Hackaday traces the history of RS-232, still everywhere in electronics.
Standardized in 1960, RS-232 sends bits one at a time over a wire ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/11/18/serial-the-port-that-wont-die/)). The "9600 8N1" setting means:

- 9,600 bits per second;
- 8 data bits, no parity bit;
- 1 stop bit.

The trap: RS-232 uses roughly ±12 V. A microcontroller runs at 3.3 V or 5 V and can be fried by a direct connection. On Linux, a USB-serial adapter shows up as `/dev/ttyUSB0`.
:::

::: details **History of x86** : Pikuma tells how x86 was born, from the 4004 to the 486.
The [Pikuma](https://pikuma.com/blog/history-of-x86-4004-to-486) article starts with Intel's 4004, released in 1971. It goes up to the 1989 486, the first x86 with over a million transistors. Concepts covered:

- memory segmentation (8086);
- protected mode, which isolates programs (286);
- paging, the basis of virtual memory (386);
- instruction pipelining (486).

Many of today's quirks exist only for compatibility.
:::

### Games and 3D

::: details **Genie 4** : DeepMind's playable world model opens to Google AI Ultra subscribers.
Shown to a few testers in late October ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-deepmind-genie-4-world-model)), Genie 4 has been available in Project Genie since November 18 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/genie-4-now-available-project-genie/)). Pro subscribers can join a waitlist. The model generates each frame from previous ones and player actions, with no physics engine. It changes weather or adds characters from text, generates audio and shares worlds in a gallery. Google acknowledges camera latency and objects drifting over long sessions.

::: details **Unity buys Synty** : Unity acquires Synty Studios and its 60,000-plus low-poly assets.
The acquisition was announced on November 16, with no price disclosed ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/synty-studios-acquisition)). Synty sells packs of objects, characters and environments widely used by indie games. Unity promises existing licenses stay valid and free assets stay free. Packs will still be sold for Unreal and Godot. The library will feed Unity Spark, an upcoming AI game creation tool, in closed beta by the end of the year.
:::

::: details **Godot and Manus AI** : the company behind the Manus agent becomes a platinum sponsor of the Godot Foundation.
The [Godot Foundation](https://godotengine.org/article/partners_manus_sponsorship_2026_announcement/) announced it on November 17, without disclosing the amount. Manus AI makes an AI agent that "turns ideas into finished work". Its co-founder hopes people who build a first game with Manus will then discover Godot. JetBrains, maker of Rider, remains a platinum sponsor of this free and open-source engine.

::: details **PixiJS 3D** : the PixiJS 2D library launches a 3D extension in open beta.
PixiJS, used by GDevelop and RPG Maker among others, releases PixiJS 3D under the MIT license ([PixiJS blog](https://pixijs.com/blog/pixi-3d-open-beta)). A 3D scene slots in as a simple element of the 2D scene. It supports glTF models, shadows, particles and several rendering styles, including a cartoon style. Rendering uses WebGPU first, with WebGL2 as a fallback. Install with `npm install pixi.js @pixi/3d`.
:::

::: details **Meta and VR** : Meta is funding more than $120 million in third-party VR games.
Meta closed three of its in-house VR studios in January. Its head of games, Chris Pruett, says the company still funds more than $120 million in games at independent studios ([UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-has-over-120m-in-funding-for-third-party-vr-games-in-active-development/)). Meta stays a publisher and funder, but produces less itself. The post also gives developers a 12-month roadmap.

### Industry and research

::: details **Palo Alto Networks and Applied Compute** : a $4.5 billion deal for an open-weight model specialist.
Applied Compute helps companies adapt and run open-weight models such as Llama or Qwen ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001327567/000132756726000030/ex992pressrelease_november.htm)). The deal is paid entirely in stock. Closing is expected by mid-2027, subject to regulatory approval. Applied Compute's CEO will become co-head of AI at Palo Alto Networks.
:::

::: details **NVIDIA and China** : NVIDIA stops selling its H200 chips in China.
Interim chief Colette Kress announced it at the first earnings call since Jensen Huang's departure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-is-abandoning-china-after-jensen-huangs-ouster/)). These sales went through an exemption to US restrictions. NVIDIA is creating an export compliance committee. According to Jefferies, new US rules on remote chip access could cost between $44 and $62 billion in revenue next year. NVIDIA says demand elsewhere will make up for it.
:::

::: details **QuEra and Aquila** : up to 180 neutral atoms used to observe a dynamical phase transition.
QuEra and Berkeley Lab published the study in Nature ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/18/quera-berkeley-lab-study-quantum-dynamics-aquila/)). The Aquila machine uses laser-trapped atoms in analog mode: the atoms evolve naturally and the team observes. The team saw an abrupt change in behavior with no equivalent in equilibrium systems. This kind of computation quickly becomes out of reach for classical computers. A supercomputer verified the results at small sizes.
:::

::: details **Quantum position verification** : NIST proves a location using entangled photons.
Two stations 195 meters apart send entangled photons and random numbers to a third, then measure the response time. The speed of light prevents an impostor elsewhere from answering in time. The photons cannot be copied. Real-world use is estimated to be more than ten years away. Preprinted in January ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16892)), the Nature publication is reported only by [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/18/quantum-physics-can-verify-a-persons-location-nist-researchers-show/).
:::

## Analysis

Nginx and Ruby share a common thread: memory management errors in old C code, including a bug about 11 years old. In Ruby's case, some of the flaws were found with Anthropic, in an engine already patched a month earlier. For teams, this means more fixes to apply quickly, especially on exposed components such as a reverse proxy. The ingress-nginx case is also a reminder that an archived project no longer gets fixes.

On the AI side, OpenAI and Google compete as much on price per task as on scores, but those scores are published by the vendors themselves. It remains to be seen whether independent evaluations will confirm the announced gaps. For an assistant like [NEXUS](/nexus), which relies on Gemini and Claude, how these prices evolve is worth watching.
