This edition is dominated by central control points that either failed or are tightening: a vendor cloud abused, an open source domain suspended and Android distribution locked down further. On the embedded side, Arduino-ESP32 ships a major release.

## Key points

::: details **GL.iNet and GoodCloud** : a flaw in the vendor's cloud service was used to plant malware on routers and KVM devices.
GL.iNet makes small OpenWrt routers and "KVM over IP" boxes that control a computer remotely, even during boot. GoodCloud is its remote management service for these devices. On November 20, the vendor found that a flaw in this service had been exploited.

According to [GL.iNet's official notice](https://forum.gl-inet.com/t/2026-11-20-gl-inet-security-notice-goodcloud-kvm-routers/73056):

- the attack path is blocked, but infected devices are not cleaned;
- a new firmware is required;
- GL-MT3000 and GL-MT6000: version 4.11.1;
- KVM RM1, RM10 and RM4PE: version 1.10.3;
- KVM RMQ1: version 1.8.7;
- indicator of compromise: a process containing `crond -i /tmp/.log`.

Researchers quoted by [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-glinet-cloud-flaw-to-infect-routers-kvm-devices/) and [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/gl-inet-cloud-zero-day-exploited/) saw admin access opened to a single IP address. GL.iNet has not confirmed this.

The vendor has not disclosed the exploitation method, the number of affected customers or any data theft. It is still checking whether computers attached to infected KVMs were compromised.

> **For you:** on a GL.iNet device, look for that process with `ps | grep tmp` over SSH, install the fixed firmware and disable cloud management if you do not use it.
:::

::: details **Android developer verification** : from January 2027, Google extends it worldwide, including to apps installed outside the Play Store.
An Android app is signed with its developer's key, which could so far stay anonymous. Verification ties that key to an identity checked by Google. It has applied since September 30 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand.

Announced on November 19 ([official page](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification)):

- global rollout starting January 2027;
- first distributors: Google Play, Galaxy Store, Honor, Oppo, Xiaomi, Vivo and Transsion;
- developers are urged to register before March 1, 2027;
- checks run through Google Play services, on certified devices running Android 8 or later;
- per Google, already registered apps account for over 99% of installs.

According to [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/google-to-make-sideloading-harder-android-developer-checks-january/), allowing unverified apps will require an "advanced" flow with a reboot and a 24-hour wait. Installing via ADB is unchanged.

Google targets scams where a fraudster walks a victim through installing an app over the phone. The "Keep Android Open" campaign sees Google becoming the gatekeeper of all software. Antitrust investigations are reported in several countries ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-android-developer-verification-january-2027/)).

> **For you:** if you distribute an Android app, even outside the Play Store, register on the Android Developer Console before March 1, 2027.
:::

::: details **Arduino-ESP32 4.0** : the new major release moves to ESP-IDF 6.1.1 and brings two changes that break without compile errors.
Arduino-ESP32 is the Arduino layer on top of ESP-IDF, Espressif's official SDK. Version 4.0.0 shipped on November 19. It moves from ESP-IDF 5.5 to 6.1.1 and supports the RISC-V based ESP32-S31.

The silent changes flagged by [DEV Community](https://dev.to/electrodai/arduino-esp32-40-is-out-the-two-changes-that-can-quietly-break-your-sketch-3165):

- PWM: for `ledcWrite()` at 8 bits, the "always on" value becomes 256;
- with 255, the output is no longer fully on, which matters for a relay or a transistor;
- `analogWrite()` is unchanged;
- web server: URLs over 2,048 bytes are rejected (414);
- headers over 4,096 bytes are rejected (431);
- slow clients are dropped (408), paths containing `..` are rejected.

Other additions: timers accept lambdas and DNS works over IPv6. Version 3.3.13, released on November 17, stays on the older ESP-IDF ([release notes](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/releases)).

> **For you:** before migrating, search for `ledcWrite` calls passing the maximum value of their resolution, such as 255 at 8 bits, and test on a separate git branch.
:::

::: details **Codeberg** : registrar NameSilo suspended the host's domain without explanation, taking down the site and git commands.
Codeberg is a non-profit code host and a GitHub alternative. On November 18, NameSilo put its domain on "clientHold", which removes it from DNS. The servers kept running, but nobody could reach them.

Facts reported by [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/codeberg-domain-suspended-by-namesilo-disrupting-open-source-projects/), the single source for details, based on Codeberg's posts:

- the domain had not expired and the account showed no suspicious logins;
- NameSilo acknowledged the suspension and referred it to its abuse team, giving no reason;
- `git clone`, `fetch` and `push` stopped working;
- hosted projects include Forgejo, Zig, GNU Guix, Libreboot, and Gentoo, which is migrating there from GitHub;
- no data was compromised, according to Codeberg.

Switching to the .eu domain was considered, then dropped. It would have meant changing two-factor setups, repository URLs and search indexing. No official announcement has confirmed the end of the suspension ([Codeberg status account](https://social.anoxinon.de/@codebergstatus/117636437840688845)).

> **For you:** add a second git remote pointing to a mirror hosted elsewhere and check your domains' status with `whois`.
:::

## Other news

Security and low-level work dominate the rest of the news. Tails pulled uBlock Origin after an XSS flaw, while a bootable ZIP and an anti-censorship AI both exploit two readings of the same bytes. Rust Coreutils nears parity with GNU and Libreboot relaxes its rule on AI-generated code. On the industry side, data center backlash, a quiet AWS outage and Mars hardware issues stand out.

### AI

::: details **CircumVolve** : GPT-5.1 generates censorship bypasses at a median cost under one dollar.
Six researchers (UMass Amherst, University of Colorado Boulder, Great Firewall Report) presented CircumVolve at ACM CCS. GPT-5.1 writes a Python program that alters packets, tests it against a censor, then refines it. Techniques found include rewritten TCP/IP headers, DNS queries misread by filters and modified initial QUIC packets. Targets were simulated censors and rented servers in China, Iran, Pakistan and Kazakhstan. Single source: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/23/1-of-gpt-51-tokens-finds-new-ways-around-chinas-great-firewall/5311532); the [paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3830454.3846759) was not reviewed.
:::

::: details **ChatGPT for SNES** : a 2.2-million-parameter model runs on a Super Nintendo.
Journalist Benj Edwards built this homemade model, unrelated to any OpenAI product. It was trained on conversations written by gpt-oss-120b and computes replies on the Super FX 2 chip. A "fact book" edition adds the opening lines of about 150,000 Wikipedia articles through the MSU-1 extension. The author calls it "not a useful assistant" and says AI agents wrote most of the code. [Project page](https://www.bxfoundry.com/chatgpt_snes/) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/21/chatgpt-shrunk-down-to-run-on-a-super-nintendo/5311418)
:::

::: details **OpenAI and perplexity** : OpenAI's chief scientist proposes a shared threshold to constrain models.
Jakub Pachocki suggests companies commit not to cross a perplexity threshold until they can better understand and monitor their models. Perplexity measures how "surprised" a model is by the next word. He cites 0.91 for GPT-4. Below 0.8, cybersecurity and math skills would emerge. The unit is not specified, and the rule comes from an interested company. Single source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/22/openais-chief-scientist-has-thoughts-on-how-to-limit-ai-development/).
:::

### Security

::: details **Tails 7.17.1** : uBlock Origin removed after a one-click universal XSS flaw.
Released urgently on November 17, the update fixes a critical flaw in the extension bundled with Tor Browser. One click on a booby-trapped site could inject code into sites visited afterwards. The "Standard" and "Safer" levels were affected, but not "Safest", which disables JavaScript. No real-world attack was observed. The extension will return once fixed. [Tails announcement](https://tails.net/news/version_7.17.1/) · [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/tails-7-17-1-emergency-update-removes-ublock-origin-over-security-flaw/)
:::

::: details **FBI and NCIC** : investigation into an apparent breach of a database shared by US police.
According to CNN and AP, cited by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/trump-says-reported-fbi-database-hack-is-not-good/), the scope of the intrusion is unknown. NCIC holds arrest warrants and background check data, among others. A former FBI official is mostly worried about data integrity: altered records could drive police actions. The FBI has published nothing and the attackers remain unknown. Donald Trump called it "not good".
:::

### Low level and hardware

::: details **A ZIP that boots a PC** : a valid archive that doubles as an x86 boot image.
David Oberhollenzer, author of squashfs-tools-ng, built this polyglot. The leading "PK" bytes decode as harmless CPU instructions. A header's date field hides a jump to the real code, and a Multiboot header lets GRUB or QEMU launch it. The boot code fits in 817 bytes, enables the A20 line and switches to 32-bit protected mode. The author calls it a proof of concept. [Author's post](https://goliath32.com/blog/ziphead.html) · [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/a-zip-archive-that-boots-your-pc-is-now-a-reality/)
:::

::: details **Rust Coreutils 0.13** : uutils reaches 96.7% compatibility with the GNU test suite.
The project rewrites core Linux commands in Rust and has been Ubuntu's default since 25.10.

- 684 of 707 GNU tests pass;
- safer symlink handling in `cp` and `mv`;
- `cut` about 23% faster;
- Base58 encoding 35 times faster;
- 82 changes sent upstream to GNU.

[Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/rust-coreutils-0-13-reaches-96-percent-gnu-compatibility/) · [release notes](https://github.com/uutils/coreutils/releases/tag/0.13.0)
:::

::: details **Libreboot** : the ban on AI-generated code becomes a reduction policy.
Libreboot, a free firmware replacing BIOS or UEFI, builds on coreboot. Its 26.11 release banned all AI-generated code. But coreboot accepts AI-assisted code, and removing all of it would force Libreboot to maintain heavy patched copies. Such code will be removed or rewritten where possible, with exceptions. One ThinkPad management function was removed this way. [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/libreboot-reverses-ai-code-ban-adopts-reduction-policy/) · [Libreboot announcement](https://libreboot.org/news/clankers.html)
:::

- six models, from 64 to 256 cores;
- 400 to 600 W power draw;
- up to 8 TB of memory;

::: details **BAE passive radar** : detecting drones and aircraft without emitting anything.
The system compares the direct signal from existing transmitters (TV, radio, mobile) with its echo off the target. It reports speed, heading, range and altitude, and fits in a 4x4. BAE says it costs up to 10 times less than many existing systems, with no independent test published. No range or accuracy figures are given. Trials are planned in 2027. [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/military/uk-ghost-radar-drones-aircraft) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/11/22/passive-ghost-radar-can-detect-airborne-threats-without-revealing-its-own-position/5311023) · [BAE Systems](https://www.baesystems.com/en/product/passive-radar)

## Analysis

Three stories hinge on a single actor controlling access: the GoodCloud service, Codeberg's registrar and Google Play services. In each case, one decision or one flaw affects devices, code or apps at scale. Mapping these dependencies (remote access, domains, distribution channels) becomes an architecture concern in its own right.

Arduino-ESP32 4.0 shows that a major release can change behavior without any compiler warning. It remains to be seen how fast third-party libraries will follow, especially for the ESP32-S31. The bootable ZIP and CircumVolve illustrate the same mechanism: two programs reading the same bytes differently.

For [NEXUS](/nexus), an Android assistant built with React Native and still in alpha, the developer verification timeline sets a concrete milestone. Its exact effect on distribution outside the Play Store remains to be clarified.
